System Design Breakdowns

Nov 2026 • 6 articles • system design • interviews

Six system design problems worked end to end, in the shape a 45–60 minute interview takes. Most are framed around a large online game platform: a virtual currency, a marketplace, multiplayer matches, friends, and the release tooling behind it all.

Every breakdown follows the same structure:

  1. Understanding the problem: functional requirements (above and below the line), non-functional requirements, and the numbers to state.

  2. The set-up: core entities and the API.

  3. High-level design: built one requirement at a time.

  4. Potential deep dives: each compared as a bad, good, and great solution.

  5. Final design: one diagram tying it together.

  6. What's expected at each level: mid-level, senior, and staff+.

01

Delayed Payment

Schedule a currency transfer for a future time and run it exactly once.

 02

Release and Deployment Pipeline

Take a merged commit to production across regions, with canaries and automatic rollback.

 03

Like / Unlike Counter

Exact like counts for millions of items at 1M reads/s and 100K writes/s.

 04

Multiplayer Matchmaking

Group players of similar skill into matches of 16 and put them on a nearby server.

 05

Friends-Played-This-Game Counter

Show how many of your friends played a game, plus a live playing-now count.

 06

URL Shortener

Short links, fast redirects, and collision-free code generation.

How to use these

Read the problem statement, close the page, and design it yourself on a whiteboard for 35 minutes. Then compare against the breakdown, paying the most attention to the deep dives: that is where interviews are won or lost. Say your numbers out loud, name concrete tables and keys, and offer the interviewer a menu of deep dives once the basics are done.
